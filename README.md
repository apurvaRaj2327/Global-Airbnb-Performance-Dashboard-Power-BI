# Global-Airbnb-Performance-Dashboard-Power-BI

## 📊 Project Overview

The **Global Airbnb Performance Dashboard** is an interactive **Power BI dashboard** designed to analyze Airbnb's global marketplace performance across listings, cities, hosts, property types, reviews, ratings, seasonality, and host trust.

The dashboard transforms Airbnb data into interactive business insights, helping users understand **market concentration, listing growth, pricing differences, customer review behavior, city-level ratings, seasonal demand, and host verification patterns**.

---

## 🎯 Project Objective

The primary objective of this project is to analyze Airbnb's global performance and answer important business questions such as:

* How has the number of Airbnb listings changed over time?
* Which cities have the largest share of Airbnb listings?
* Which cities generate the highest share of reviews?
* How do property types differ in average price?
* Which cities have the highest and lowest ratings?
* How frequently do customers leave reviews?
* How does review activity change throughout the year?
* What proportion of hosts provide trust signals such as profile pictures and identity verification?

# 📈 Dashboard Analysis

## 1. New Listings & Airbnb Growth

The first dashboard page analyzes Airbnb's listing growth over time from **2008 to 2020**.

The analysis divides Airbnb's development into different stages:

* Introduction
* Growth
* Maturity
* Decline
* Reinvention
* COVID-19

The dashboard highlights that **2015 represented the highest point for new listings**. It also identifies a period of restraint during 2016–2017 and subsequent growth before the COVID-19 disruption.

The visualization also compares different property types, including:

* Entire Place
* Hotel Room
* Private Room
* Shared Room

This provides a historical view of how Airbnb's property mix evolved over time.

---

# 🌍 2. Market Share by City

The market-share analysis compares Airbnb activity across:

* Paris
* New York
* Sydney
* Rome
* Rio de Janeiro
* Istanbul
* Mexico City
* Bangkok
* Cape Town
* Hong Kong

One of the key findings is that **Paris, New York, and Sydney account for almost half of total listings and approximately 59% of total reviews**.

Paris has the largest number of listings and reviews among the cities analyzed.

The dashboard also uses a cumulative percentage visualization to show how Airbnb listings are distributed across cities.

---

# 💰 3. Average Price by Property Type

The dashboard compares average prices across four major property categories:

| Property Type | Average Price |
| ------------- | ------------: |
| Hotel Room    |          $800 |
| Entire Place  |          $673 |
| Shared Room   |          $580 |
| Private Room  |          $462 |

The analysis highlights a substantial difference in average pricing between property types, with **hotel rooms showing the highest average price** and private rooms the lowest among the categories presented.

---

# ⭐ 4. City Rating Analysis

The rating section evaluates cities using multiple customer-rating dimensions:

* Accuracy
* Cleanliness
* Communication
* Location
* Value

The dashboard identifies **Mexico City and Rio de Janeiro as the overall best-rated cities**, while **Hong Kong and Istanbul have comparatively lower ratings**.

The analysis also identifies **cleanliness and value for money** as two areas that generally receive lower scores compared with other rating dimensions.

The detailed rating table allows comparison of individual cities across all rating categories.

---

# 👥 5. Review Frequency Analysis

The review-frequency analysis examines how often customers leave reviews.

A major finding is that **86.5% of reviewers left one review**, while **98.8% of customers wrote three reviews or fewer**.

The dashboard also highlights an unusual case where one customer appears to have written **283 reviews**, associated with two Bangkok listings. The dashboard flags this as a potential data-quality issue or an unusual customer behavior pattern.

This section demonstrates how review-frequency analysis can also be used for **data-quality investigation**.

---

# 📅 6. Seasonality Analysis

The seasonality visualization analyzes the monthly distribution of reviews across:

* Mexico City
* New York
* Paris
* Rome
* Sydney

Key observations include:

* **Paris and Rome dominate review share from April to August**, reflecting stronger European summer travel activity.
* **New York shows increased review activity during November and December**, consistent with the holiday season.

This helps demonstrate how Airbnb activity can vary according to geographic location and time of year.

---

# 🛡️ 7. Host Trust & Verification

The dashboard also evaluates host trust indicators using:

* Profile Picture
* Identity Verification

The analysis shows that:

* **66.9%** of hosts have a profile picture and verified identity.
* **32.6%** have a profile picture but are not identity verified.
* Only **0.1%** are identity verified without a profile picture.
* **0.3%** have neither profile picture nor identity verification.

This indicates that the large majority of hosts provide at least one visible trust signal.

---

# 💡 Key Business Insights

### 🏙️ Market Concentration

Paris, New York, and Sydney represent a significant portion of Airbnb listings and reviews, indicating strong marketplace concentration in major global destinations.

### 💰 Pricing Differences

Average prices vary considerably by property type, with hotel rooms having the highest average price in the analyzed dataset.

### ⭐ Customer Experience

Mexico City and Rio de Janeiro show particularly strong overall rating performance, while Hong Kong and Istanbul score comparatively lower.

### 📅 Seasonal Demand

Review activity demonstrates clear seasonal patterns, particularly across European destinations and the New York holiday period.

### 👥 Customer Engagement

Most customers leave relatively few reviews, with nearly all reviewers contributing three or fewer reviews.

### 🛡️ Host Trust

Most hosts provide visible trust signals through profile pictures and/or identity verification.

### 🔎 Data Quality

The unusually high review count associated with one customer provides an example of how dashboard analysis can reveal potential data anomalies requiring further investigation.
